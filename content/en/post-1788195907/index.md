## What Changed in 2026 — And Why It Matters

Meanwhile, intro pricing across the board remains aggressively low, and renewal rates tell a completely different story. That gap between what you pay in year one and year two is the single most important number to understand before you commit.

## The Honest Truth About What Most Reviews Won't Tell You

The intro-to-renewal price gap is not a minor detail. It's the single biggest source of frustration among WordPress site owners I've spoken with. Every provider in this list charges dramatically less in year one than in subsequent years. SiteGround nearly 6x's its price. Ionos does 12x. Even Hostinger roughly quadruples. This isn't a scam — it's the standard business model — but you need to factor renewal pricing into your decision, not just the headline number.

Another thing the roundup articles gloss over: "managed" means different things at every host. SiteGround's managed tier includes auto-updates, caching, and expert support. WP Engine's includes all that plus staging, CDN, and hardened security. At the budget end, "managed" often just means they installed WordPress for you. Read the fine print.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between web hosting and WordPress hosting?**
Web hosting is the general term for server space that houses any website. WordPress hosting is optimized specifically for WordPress — it includes one-click installs, auto-updates for the CMS, and server configurations tuned for PHP and MySQL performance. The overlap is growing (most providers now call everything "WordPress hosting"), but the depth of optimization varies enormously.

**How much should I pay for WordPress hosting in 2026?**
Non-managed plans run $3–10/month at launch and $10–20/month at renewal, according to CNET. Fully managed plans for high-traffic or multi-site setups start at $20+/month. The price you see in the advertisement is almost never the price you'll pay in year two and beyond.

**Which host is actually the fastest?**
CNET's benchmarks put GoDaddy at 2.29 seconds average and Hostinger at 2.34 seconds. But raw speed depends heavily on your server location, theme, and plugin stack. A bloated site on a fast server will still load slowly.

**Should I choose managed or non-managed WordPress hosting?**
If you're running a business site, managing client sites, or simply don't want to deal with updates, caching, and security, managed is worth the premium. If you're a developer or comfortable with server administration, non-managed gives you more control at a lower cost.

**Is the AI integration worth it?**
For 2026, mostly no — but it's evolving fast. Hostinger's Kodee and Ionos's AI generator are useful for drafting content and setting up basic site structures. They're not replacements for a strategist or a designer. Treat them as helpful copilots, not autopilots.
