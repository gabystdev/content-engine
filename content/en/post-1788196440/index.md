## Does a VPN Actually Slow Down Streaming? The Numbers Say No — If You Pick the Right Protocol

Here is the fact that should end the debate: a modern WireGuard-based VPN on a nearby server causes only 3–15% speed loss — often imperceptible. OpenVPN-based VPNs retain 40–45% of base speed; WireGuard-based ones retain 85–95%. NordLynx (NordVPN) and WireGuard (Surfshark, Mullvad) are the fastest protocols across every reviewer that tested protocol-level splits.

The slowest connection recorded in DigitalShieldPro testing — a route to Australia delivering 510 Mbps — was still 20x the 25 Mbps threshold required for 4K streaming. In practical terms, any VPN in this top 10 will handle 4K, HDR, and even 8K streams without buffering, provided you pick a server within a reasonable geographic distance.

## Which Protocol Should You Choose for Streaming?

WireGuard-based protocols are the answer across every review published between 2025 and August 2026. NordLynx (NordVPN's WireGuard-based protocol) delivered the highest speeds in testing. Surfshark's WireGuard implementation and Mullvad's native WireGuard support both retained 85%+ of base speed. If your VPN app gives you a protocol choice, select WireGuard or the vendor's WireGuard-based variant for streaming.

## Is It Legal to Use a VPN for Streaming?

Using a VPN is legal in most countries, but streaming platforms' terms of service prohibit accessing content from outside your subscribed region. No reviewer in this dataset — CNET, Top10VPN, Cloudwards, Security.org — flagged legal risk for end users in Western markets. The risk, to the extent one exists, falls on the streaming provider's enforcement (account restriction), not on the VPN user. This varies by jurisdiction; users outside the US, EU, UK, Canada, Australia, and Japan should check local law.
